**IDDSI Level 4–5 | 30 minutes | Moderate**

Glutinous rice balls (湯丸 / 湯圓, tong yuen) are one of the most symbolically important desserts in Chinese food culture, served at the Winter Solstice (冬至) and Lantern Festival (元宵節) to represent family unity. This adapted version uses mini-sized rice balls with sesame filling, cooked significantly longer than standard instructions to achieve a fully softened texture that meets IDDSI Level 4–5. Served in warm ginger syrup, it allows elderly residents with dysphagia to participate in an important cultural and festive tradition without compromising on safety.

## Ingredients (4 servings)

**Rice balls:**
- 200g store-bought mini sesame glutinous rice balls (approx. 1.5cm diameter)

**Ginger syrup:**
- 30g fresh old ginger (老薑), peeled and sliced
- 70g yellow rock sugar (片糖)
- 800ml water
- 2 pandan leaves (optional, for fragrance)

## Method

**Ginger syrup:**
1. Combine water, ginger slices and pandan leaves (if using) in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, then reduce to low and simmer for 10 minutes.
2. Add rock sugar and stir until dissolved. Strain and keep warm.

**Rice balls:**
3. Bring a separate pot of water to a boil. Add mini rice balls.
4. Once the balls float to the surface, continue cooking for a further 5 minutes — approximately 2–3 minutes longer than package instructions — to ensure thorough softening throughout.
5. Remove with a slotted spoon and transfer directly into the warm ginger syrup.

**Serving:**
6. Place 4–5 rice balls per bowl and ladle ginger syrup over. Serve immediately while hot.

## Texture Test

**CRITICAL — test every batch individually:**

Press each rice ball with a spoon using moderate force — it should flatten easily and not spring back (Level 4–5). If the ball bounces back or resists flattening, continue cooking for 2–3 more minutes and retest.

**Important size limit:** Rice balls must be no larger than 1.5cm in diameter. Standard-sized rice balls (2.5–3cm) pose a choking hazard and are not appropriate for residents with dysphagia.

## Safety Notes

⚠️ **Size is critical** — use only mini rice balls (approximately 1.5cm diameter). Do not use standard-sized tong yuen.

⚠️ **Mandatory texture test** — glutinous rice cooking time varies with heat and batch size. Always test by pressing with a spoon before serving; never rely solely on cook time.

⚠️ **Natural stickiness** — glutinous rice retains natural adhesive properties even when fully cooked. Residents requiring Level 3 or below should not consume this dish; the rice ball component should be omitted and only the ginger syrup served.

⚠️ **Digestibility** — glutinous rice is slower to digest than regular rice. Residents with gastrointestinal conditions should consume in small quantities.

## Nutrition

Approximately 200 kcal per serving (4–5 balls with syrup), 38g carbohydrates, 6g fat from sesame filling. Sesame is rich in calcium, iron and unsaturated fatty acids; the dish provides moderate energy from carbohydrates and meaningful calcium from the sesame filling. Well-suited as a festive dessert supplement to a regular meal.

## Cultural Note

Tong yuen (湯丸/湯圓) carry profound cultural weight in Cantonese and wider Chinese tradition. Their name is a near-homophone of 團圓 (family reunion), making them inseparable from the imagery of togetherness at the Winter Solstice. For elderly residents in care settings — many of whom have celebrated this festival with family for decades — the ability to share a bowl of tong yuen, even in adapted form, is not a trivial accommodation. This recipe was designed specifically so that residents with dysphagia can remain part of that shared table at festive gatherings, with dignity and safety preserved equally.
