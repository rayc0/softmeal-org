**IDDSI Level 3 (Liquidised) | 35 minutes | Easy**

粟米雞肉羹 (corn chicken thick soup) is a beloved Cantonese soup that combines the natural sweetness of corn with the mild flavour of chicken. This version blends the corn completely and incorporates fine chicken puree to create a smooth, flowing Level 3 consistency. It is rich in natural sugars, B vitamins and complete protein — an ideal choice for elderly residents who need both nutritional density and easy swallowing in every bowl.

## Ingredients (3–4 servings)

**Main:**
- 2 fresh corn cobs (or 400g frozen sweet corn kernels)
- 200g chicken breast, roughly chopped
- 1 litre clear chicken stock (low sodium)
- 1 egg white, lightly beaten
- 1 teaspoon sesame oil

**Seasoning:**
- Salt to taste
- White pepper to taste
- 1 teaspoon cornstarch dissolved in 2 tablespoons cold water (optional, for thickening)

## Method

1. If using fresh corn, strip the kernels from the cob using a sharp knife. Place kernels in a small saucepan with 300ml of the chicken stock; bring to a boil and simmer for 10 minutes until completely tender.
2. Transfer cooked corn kernels and stock to a blender; blend on high speed for 90 seconds until completely smooth.
3. Pass through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean pot, pressing the solids firmly to extract all liquid; discard corn fibre residue.
4. In a separate small pot, poach the chicken breast in water or stock for 10 minutes until fully cooked. Transfer to a blender with 100ml stock; blend to a fine smooth paste, then pass through the sieve.
5. Combine the corn liquid and chicken paste in a medium saucepan with the remaining stock. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat.
6. If a thicker consistency is needed, stir in the dissolved cornstarch gradually while stirring continuously; simmer for 2 minutes.
7. Season with salt, white pepper and sesame oil. Drizzle in the egg white while stirring slowly in one direction to create fine, dispersed strands throughout the soup.
8. Confirm consistency: the soup should flow continuously when a ladle is tilted. Adjust with more stock if too thick.

## Texture Test

**IDDSI Level 3 confirmation:** When a spoon is tilted, the soup flows slowly but continuously, coating the spoon in a thin layer. No corn fibre particles are present. Egg white strands should be very fine and fully dispersed — no pieces larger than 3mm. IDDSI syringe test: 1–10ml expelled over 10 seconds.

## Safety Notes

**Egg white check** — pour egg white slowly in a thin stream while stirring; if strands form that are larger than 3mm, pass through the sieve again before serving.

**Corn fibre** — sieving is mandatory. Any remaining corn fibre fragments can obstruct the airway in people with reduced swallowing function.

**Stock sodium** — use homemade or low-sodium commercial stock for residents with hypertension or kidney disease.

## Sourcing in Hong Kong

- **Fresh sweet corn**: Wet market vegetable stalls; available year-round, with peak season March–August
- **Chicken breast**: PARKnSHOP, Wellcome or wet market poultry stalls — choose locally sourced fresh breast for best flavour
- **Frozen corn kernels**: Convenient alternative from supermarket frozen sections; nutritionally comparable to fresh

## Nutrition

Approximately 145 kcal per serving (250ml), 14g protein, 18g carbohydrates. The combination of corn's natural sugars and chicken's complete amino acid profile provides sustained energy and muscle-supporting protein. Corn is a good source of lutein and zeaxanthin, which support eye health — particularly relevant for elderly residents at risk of macular degeneration.

## Cultural Note

Corn chicken thick soup (粟米雞肉羹) is a standard offering on the Cantonese menu of Hong Kong restaurant chains and hospital canteens alike. Its mild, sweet character and smooth texture have made it a comfort food across generations — and the fact that its natural ingredients blend to a safe dysphagia consistency with minimal modification makes it one of the most accessible bridges between everyday Cantonese eating culture and clinical dietary requirements.
