**IDDSI Level 5 (Minced & Moist) | 20 minutes | Easy**

芝士焗蛋 (cheese baked egg) is a gently baked egg preparation in which beaten egg is combined with a small amount of cream and seasoning, poured into a ramekin, topped with grated cheese, and baked in a water bath until the surface is lightly golden and the interior is just-set and yielding. The water bath (bain-marie) is the critical step: it ensures gentle, even heat distribution that prevents over-cooking and keeps the egg texture at a soft, yielding Level 5 consistency. Melted cheese adds flavour, fat and additional protein.

## Ingredients (2 servings)

**Main:**
- 3 large eggs
- 2 tablespoons single cream or full-fat milk
- 30g grated cheese (cheddar, Emmental or Gruyère)
- 1 teaspoon unsalted butter (for greasing ramekins)

**Seasoning:**
- Pinch of salt
- White pepper to taste
- Pinch of paprika or dried thyme (optional, for flavour)

## Method

1. Preheat oven to 160°C (fan-assisted 150°C). Grease two 150ml ramekins with butter.
2. Break eggs into a bowl; add cream, salt, white pepper and herbs/spices if using. Beat gently until combined — do not over-beat (you want to avoid excess air bubbles).
3. Pour egg mixture evenly into the two greased ramekins, filling to approximately 80% capacity.
4. Scatter grated cheese evenly over the surface.
5. Place ramekins in a deep baking dish. Pour boiling water into the baking dish until it reaches halfway up the sides of the ramekins (bain-marie / water bath).
6. Bake in the preheated oven for 15–18 minutes until the surface is lightly golden and the egg is just-set — it should jiggle slightly in the centre but not be liquid.
7. Remove from oven; lift ramekins out of water bath carefully. Rest for 2 minutes before serving.

## Texture Test

**IDDSI Level 5 (Minced & Moist) confirmation:** The baked egg should be soft throughout and separate into small, moist pieces under the side of a fork with minimal pressure. No knife is required. The cheese topping should be fully melted and integrated into the surface. The interior should be moist, not rubbery — there should be no rebound when pressed with a fork.

**If the texture is too firm** (rubbery or springy), the egg was over-cooked. Reduce baking time by 2 minutes next time, or reduce oven temperature.

## Safety Notes

**Water bath is mandatory** — baking without a water bath will over-cook the exterior before the interior sets, producing a rubbery Level 6+ texture.

**Checking doneness** — gently shake the ramekin; the egg should jiggle slightly at the centre but not flow. If it flows freely, return to oven for 2 more minutes.

**Cheese sodium** — hard cheeses are high in sodium; use a moderate amount and do not add extra salt for residents with hypertension.

**Dairy allergy** — not suitable for residents with dairy allergy; substitute with plant-based cheese alternative and oat cream.

## Nutrition

Approximately 195 kcal per serving (one ramekin), 14g protein, 14g fat. Eggs are among the most nutrient-dense protein sources available: one serving provides meaningful amounts of all essential amino acids, plus vitamin D, B12, riboflavin, selenium and choline. Cheese adds calcium, saturated fat and additional protein. This is a high-protein, moderate-calorie preparation suitable for residents requiring protein supplementation within a small meal volume.

## Cultural Note

Baked egg (焗蛋) in various forms appears across Hong Kong's Western-influenced café culture — from hotel breakfast buffets to the humble cha chaan teng breakfast set. The addition of cheese elevates it into a dish familiar from Hong Kong's unique East-meets-West food identity. For elderly residents who grew up eating Western-style breakfasts at local cafes, this dish carries a warm, specific memory — a connection to a certain kind of Hong Kong morning that no clinical formula can recreate.
