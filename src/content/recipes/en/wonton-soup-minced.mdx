**IDDSI Level 5 (Minced & Moist) | 35 minutes | Medium**

Wonton soup is the soul of Hong Kong noodle shops — gossamer-thin wonton wrappers enveloping a delicate pork and shrimp filling, floating in a clear, deeply savoury broth. By selecting the thinnest available wonton wrappers, mincing the filling to a near-paste consistency, cutting each cooked wonton into small pieces, and ensuring complete immersion in warm broth, this dish achieves IDDSI Level 5 Minced & Moist compliance. For many elderly residents, the smell of wonton soup alone is a powerful, comforting food memory.

Wonton soup (云吞湯, wan tan tong in Cantonese) is the quintessential Hong Kong street food — a rich pork-and-shrimp dumpling in clear broth. This texture-modified version replaces whole shrimp with fine minced pork to achieve a safe IDDSI Level 5 consistency while preserving the familiar broth flavour. For international kitchens, wonton wrappers are available fresh or frozen at East Asian grocers; use Cantonese-style thin wrappers (not Shanghai-style thick), as the thinner skin reliably achieves Level 5 softness after steaming without requiring additional tenderising.

## Ingredients (2 servings, approximately 10 wontons)

**Filling:**
- 100g minced pork (half-lean, half-fat; mince until nearly paste-like)
- 60g fresh shrimp (peeled and deveined; finely minced)
- 1/2 teaspoon grated fresh ginger
- 1 teaspoon light soy sauce
- 1/4 teaspoon sugar
- A few drops of sesame oil
- 1/2 teaspoon cornstarch
- A pinch of white pepper

**Wonton wrappers:**
- 10 sheets thin-style Hong Kong wonton wrappers (choose the thinnest available)

**Broth:**
- 500ml clear chicken stock or superior broth (low-sodium)
- 2 slices fresh ginger
- Fine salt to taste

## Method

1. Mince pork and shrimp separately until nearly paste-like; combine with all seasoning; stir in one direction until the mixture becomes sticky and cohesive
2. Place 1 teaspoon of filling in the centre of each wonton wrapper; moisten the edges with a little water
3. Fold or wrap into the traditional Hong Kong wonton shape; press edges firmly to seal completely
4. Bring a large pot of water to the boil; add wontons and cook over medium heat for 4–5 minutes until wrappers are translucent and floating
5. Meanwhile, heat chicken stock with ginger slices until simmering; remove and discard ginger
6. Remove cooked wontons with a slotted spoon; transfer to serving bowls; pour hot broth over wontons
7. Using scissors or a knife, cut each wonton into 2–3 pieces (each piece must not exceed 1.5cm in any dimension)
8. Ensure all wonton pieces are fully submerged in broth — no dry surfaces; serve immediately

## Texture Test

**Fork pressure test:** Passes Level 5 — the thin, cooked wonton wrapper yields easily under the side of a fork without cutting; filling is fine-textured and soft, easily compressed by tongue pressure against the palate.

**Moisture check:** All wonton pieces must be fully immersed in warm broth with no exposed dry surfaces — a defining requirement of Level 5 Minced & Moist. Top up with additional hot broth if any pieces surface above the liquid.

## Safety Notes

⚠️ **Whole wontons do not meet Level 5 standards** — a whole wonton is too large and potentially too elastic to be safe for individuals with dysphagia. Every wonton must be cut into segments of 1.5cm or less before serving.

⚠️ **Wrapper thickness is critical** — thick-style or Shanghai-style wonton wrappers remain too firm and chewy after cooking. Only the thinnest Hong Kong-style wrappers will achieve Level 5 compliance after steaming.

⚠️ **Shrimp particle size** — shrimp has a naturally firm, springy texture; it must be minced to extremely fine particles (under 0.4cm) before inclusion in the filling. Any larger pieces must be re-minced.
