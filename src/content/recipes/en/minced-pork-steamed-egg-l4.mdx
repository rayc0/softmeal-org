**IDDSI Level 4 (Pureed) | 25 minutes | Easy | High-Protein**

Minced pork steamed egg (肉碎蒸蛋) is one of the most universally loved Cantonese home-cooked dishes — a silky, custard-textured steamed egg enriched with seasoned minced pork. It is a staple of Cantonese household cooking, appearing regularly on both family dinner tables and in cha chaan teng lunch boxes. For elderly residents with dysphagia, it is nearly ideal: the steamed egg base naturally achieves IDDSI Level 4 (Pureed) consistency when the correct egg-to-liquid ratio is used, requiring zero blending or post-cooking processing. The minced pork breaks apart with the lightest pressure and is distributed throughout the silky egg matrix, adding protein and flavour. One serving provides approximately 18g of protein — a meaningful contribution toward the 1.0–1.2g/kg/day protein target recommended for elderly residents to prevent sarcopenia.

## Ingredients (2–3 servings)

**Steamed egg:**
- 3 large eggs (approximately 55g each)
- 250ml warm water or light chicken stock (approximately 1:1.5 egg-to-liquid ratio by volume)
- ½ teaspoon light soy sauce
- ¼ teaspoon sesame oil
- Pinch of salt

**Minced pork:**
- 120g minced pork (regular fat content — lean mince produces a drier texture)
- 1 tablespoon light soy sauce
- ½ teaspoon sugar
- 1 teaspoon cornflour
- ½ teaspoon sesame oil
- ¼ teaspoon white pepper
- 1 teaspoon ginger juice (optional)

**Finishing:**
- 1 teaspoon light soy sauce (for drizzling over the finished dish)
- ½ teaspoon sesame oil
- 1 tablespoon warm water

## Method

1. Prepare the minced pork: combine minced pork with soy sauce, sugar, cornflour, sesame oil and white pepper. Mix well in one direction until slightly sticky. Set aside to marinate for 10 minutes.
2. Beat the eggs gently in a bowl — avoid over-beating, which incorporates excessive air and produces bubbles that create pitting in the finished surface. Beat just until the yolks and whites are combined.
3. Add the warm water or stock (must be warm, not hot — hot liquid begins to cook the egg before steaming), soy sauce, sesame oil and salt. Stir gently to combine. Do not beat.
4. Strain the egg mixture through a fine-mesh sieve into a shallow heatproof dish (this removes any undissolved proteins and produces a smoother surface). A wider, shallower dish produces more even steaming than a deep bowl.
5. Spread the marinated minced pork evenly over the surface of the strained egg mixture. Press gently so the pork is just below the surface, not piled high.
6. Place the dish in a steamer over medium heat. Cover with a plate or wrap the dish loosely with foil (this prevents condensation droplets from falling onto the surface and creating pitting). Steam for 12–15 minutes.
7. The egg is ready when the surface is just set and trembles gently when shaken — the centre should have a slight jiggle but no visible liquid. Do not oversteam — overcooked steamed egg becomes porous, rubbery, and unsuitable for Level 4.
8. Combine the finishing soy sauce, sesame oil and warm water in a small bowl. Drizzle over the finished steamed egg just before serving.

## Texture Test

**Fork drip test:** Passes Level 4 — a small piece placed on a fork falls off under its own weight or with very light pressure; it does not hold a firm shape when pushed through fork tines.

**Pressure test:** A piece pressed between two fingers yields with minimal resistance and breaks apart smoothly — it does not bounce back or require chewing force.

**Consistency:** The egg is uniformly smooth throughout, without holes, bubbles, or firm rubbery spots. The minced pork particles are fully cooked and distributed throughout; any single pork particle breaks with light fork pressure.

## Safety Notes

⚠️ **Do not oversteam** — oversteamed egg becomes porous and rubbery, shifting it toward a firmer texture that may no longer meet Level 4 requirements. Check at 12 minutes and test texture before serving.

⚠️ **Temperature control** — serve at 60–70°C. Steamed egg retains heat well; test temperature before serving to residents with reduced oral sensation.

⚠️ **Pork must be fully cooked** — ensure the minced pork is cooked through completely. After steaming, the pork should show no pink areas. If in doubt, cut into the pork layer to verify before serving.

⚠️ **Liquid settling** — if the dish is allowed to cool before steaming, the pork may sink and create an uneven texture. Steam immediately after assembly for best results.

## Nutrition

Approximately 190 kcal per serving, 18g protein, 12g fat, 2g carbohydrates. This dish is one of the most protein-efficient IDDSI-compliant recipes available — high-quality complete protein from both eggs (all essential amino acids) and pork, in a format that requires zero oral processing effort beyond swallowing. Particularly recommended for residents with sarcopenia risk, poor appetite, or post-illness recovery. The egg and pork combination also provides selenium, zinc, choline (important for cognitive function), vitamin D and B12.

## Cultural Note

Steamed egg is a ubiquitous comfort food across Cantonese and broader Chinese cuisine. It is what Cantonese parents make for children who are unwell, what elderly residents grew up eating, and what triggers powerful food memories for most Hong Kong seniors. The smell of sesame oil and soy sauce over freshly steamed egg is instantly recognisable and appetite-stimulating. In a care home context, steamed egg is one of the rare dishes that simultaneously meets rigorous nutritional and texture requirements while also providing genuine emotional comfort.

## Variation

- **To adjust to Level 3 (Liquidised):** Increase the liquid-to-egg ratio to approximately 2:1 (300ml liquid to 3 eggs) and steam for 10 minutes. The result will flow freely from a spoon rather than holding its shape.
- **Protein boost:** Stir 1 scoop of unflavoured whey or soy protein powder (approximately 20g) into the egg mixture before steaming — the additional protein is invisible in the finished dish and does not significantly affect texture.
- **Without pork (vegetarian):** Omit minced pork entirely; the plain steamed egg on its own is equally suitable for Level 4. Add diced silken tofu (2mm pieces) for variety.
